About the New 7 Wonders

A worldwide poll organized by the New7Wonders Foundation attracted more than 100 million votes, and the results were announced in Lisbon, Portugal on July 7, 2007.

The original Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, such as the Hanging Gardens of Babylon and the Lighthouse of Alexandria, were compiled by Greek writers, but only the Great Pyramid of Giza survives today. It holds honorary status in the new list.

Yes! All seven are major tourist destinations, and each one is also recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Great Wall of China China

Begun as early as the 7th century BC and expanded by successive dynasties, the Great Wall protected China from invasions. Contrary to popular myth, it is not visible from the Moon with the naked eye.

Petra Jordan

Flourishing from around the 5th century BC, Petra controlled the trade routes of incense and spices. Its most famous facade, Al-Khazneh (The Treasury), greets visitors after a walk through a narrow canyon called the Siq.

Christ the Redeemer Brazil

Completed in 1931, the statue is made of reinforced concrete and soapstone. It stands about 700 meters above sea level on Corcovado mountain and has become one of the most recognizable symbols of Brazil.

Machu Picchu Peru

Built around 1450 for the Inca emperor Pachacuti and abandoned a century later during the Spanish conquest, Machu Picchu was revealed to the world by Hiram Bingham in 1911. Its stones fit together without mortar.

Chichen Itza Mexico

Flourishing from around 600 AD, Chichen Itza was one of the greatest Maya centers in Yucatan. During the spring and autumn equinoxes, the light on El Castillo creates the illusion of a serpent descending its staircase.

Colosseum Italy

Opened in 80 AD under Emperor Titus, the Colosseum hosted gladiator contests, animal hunts, and even mock sea battles. Its underground passages, called the hypogeum, lifted fighters and animals onto the arena floor.

Taj Mahal India

Completed in 1653 after more than two decades of construction, the Taj Mahal combines Persian, Islamic, and Indian styles. Its marble changes color with the light of the day, and its dome rises about 73 meters high.
